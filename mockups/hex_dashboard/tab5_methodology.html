Methodology

How a provider gets flagged

Every flag in this app is a peer-relative statistical position — computed within a provider's own taxonomy-and-state cohort, using a method built to survive the heavy right skew of Medicare cost data. Three things determine whether a provider is flagged: the peer group, the score, and the coverage gate.

Defined in models/marts/mart_provider_outliers.sql · thresholds |z| ≥ 2.0, |mz| ≥ 3.5, floor n ≥ 30

In short
  • Peer groups are keyed on NPPES taxonomy code × state — not the noisy free-text CMS specialty label.
  • Flagging uses the modified z-score (MAD) at |mz| ≥ 3.5, robust to a handful of megaproviders that would distort a classical mean.
  • Each flag is gated on a per-metric peer-coverage floor of 30, so no one is judged against a thin median.
1

Peer groups

Outliers are computed within peer groups keyed on NPPES taxonomy_code × state — deliberately not the free-text CMS specialty label. The registry taxonomy is a controlled vocabulary that stays consistent across providers, whereas the specialty text is noisy and frequently mislabeled (the same role appears under a dozen spellings, and self-reported values drift year to year).

Anchoring the cohort on the taxonomy code means a provider is always measured against true clinical peers in the same state. The trade-off: the join to peer-group statistics is an inner join, so providers without NPPES coverage have no canonical_taxonomy_code and drop out of the mart entirely rather than being scored against a mismatched group.

2

Z-score vs. modified z-score (MAD)

Two scores are computed for every metric. The classical z-score measures distance from the group mean in standard deviations:

Classical z-scoreflag at |z| ≥ 2.0
z = (x − mean) / stddev

The problem: Medicare cost distributions are heavily right-skewed. A few megaproviders inflate both the mean and the standard deviation, which masks genuine outliers and makes the classical score unreliable at the tail. The modified z-score replaces the mean with the median and the standard deviation with the median absolute deviation (MAD) — both resistant to extreme values:

Modified z-score · Iglewicz & Hoaglinflag at |mz| ≥ 3.5
mz = 0.6745 · (x − median) / MAD
The constant 0.6745 rescales MAD so that, for normally distributed data, mz matches the classical z.

Both flags are additionally gated on a per-metric peer-coverage floor of 30. A peer group can clear the admission floor on its total provider count while having only a handful of actual prescribers for a given metric — so each metric's flag checks its own coverage count (peer_n_part_d, peer_n_part_b, and the brand-share and avg-cost counts) before a provider can be flagged. No one is flagged against a median built from a few peers.

3

What an outlier flag means (and doesn't)

A flag is a statistical position — a value far from its peer median — and not an allegation of fraud, abuse, or wrongdoing. Many entirely legitimate patterns produce extreme scores.

The dominant pattern in this dataset is a single attending NPI absorbing facility-level prescribing: in many emergency departments, one physician's NPI is attached to the entire department's Part D activity. That is exactly why Emergency-Medicine physicians dominate the drug-cost tail — and why the top row of the leaderboard, RUSHDI ALUL at a modified-z of 134,510, reflects a billing structure rather than any individual behavior. The flag tells you where to look, not what you'll find.
